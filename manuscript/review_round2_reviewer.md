## Overall assessment of the new experiments

(a) Adequacy and overclaiming.
- The positive control is the strongest addition: it removes the "gate accepts nothing" worry for strict scoring. It is also nearly trivial (ProTeGi prompts are the bare seed, so the exemplar helps 15 of 15), and it says nothing about the refiner.
- V2F is informative but confounded: it changes the baseline, re-attaches content in code, and has no rubric-free control. It is one seed on 44 of 45 cells.
- The lenient validation, the audit of 80 rounds, the paired V2-V3 comparison, the 8B refiner and the rescoring are all single-pass, small or one-seed. They support "scoped and descriptive" and nothing stronger. Several sentences still go beyond that: "so the rubric is not what produces them" in 5.6.1, "the picture is the same at three scales" in 5.6.2, "worked reasoning adds at most a few points" in 5.3.1, and "the guards are mostly right" (n=40).

(b) AI labelling.
- It is disclosed in Sec 5.1.1, 5.2, 6.3.3 and the AI declaration, and the data is released, which is honest. It is not disclosed in the abstract, and Sec 4.5 wrongly says "manually labelled".
- It undermines both validations because of non-independence (the same assistant family wrote the code and the parser fix), circularity (the labeller reads the same text region as the parser) and the absence of any human check or agreement statistic. It supports at best "AI-screened". The claim in 6.3.3 "precision and recall above 96%" should be qualified, or a human subsample added.

(c) Unsupported new claims.
- "Almost all of the effect is the tag" (APE yes; ProTeGi about 80%).
- "Same picture at three scales."
- "The rubric is not what produces them."
- "The guards are mostly right."
- The judge-independence claim (the 8B row is missing).
- "Gains are mostly format repairs" rests on the three ProTeGi cells plus the lenient reversal. That is plausible but not proven; the hyperbaton/PromptWizard gain is real under both metrics.

(d) Title and abstract.
- The title matches the scope better. The abstract is accurate on the main numbers but dense, omits AI-labelling, and says "prompts from three small-model optimizers" although one optimizer returned the seed in all 15 cells. A reader still cannot tell that the main negative rests on a 1.7B self-refiner.

## Recommendation: minor revision, leaning major

The paper has become an honest, scoped negative-result report, and I would accept it as such once the text defects and the overclaims are fixed. Two points are not optional. First, the unfilled "{len(l3)}" placeholder, the missing 8B judge row (with the false "not a refiner" claim), the format-line contradiction and the broken references must be fixed before publication. Second, the AI-only labelling must be softened in the claims and ideally backed by a human subsample.

The one experiment that could still change the conclusion is the format-preserving treatment (V2F), plus a rubric-free control, with a capable refiner and multiple seeds. If the authors do not add it, they must state plainly that "does rubric-guided refinement help a capable refiner" is untested. If they add it, I would accept without further rounds.
