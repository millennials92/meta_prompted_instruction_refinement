\bigskip
Dear Editor,

Thank you for the opportunity to resubmit. The reviews of Access-2026-54235 identified one central problem: the primary
treatment dropped the answer format in almost all candidates, so the null result could not show that rubric-guided
refinement fails. The revised manuscript addresses it with new experiments and a narrower scope.

\medskip
What changed. (i) A positive control: the validation gate accepted a known-good candidate in 24 of 26 cells in which it helps.
(ii) A re-run of the main grid with a format- and example-preserving treatment on 44 cells: candidates were accepted in
15 runs, but under a lenient metric the final prompts won in 1 cell(s) and lost significantly in 3.
(iii) A validation of the lenient parser on 400 labelled responses (precision 98.4\%, recall 96.8\%); the labels
and the audit of 80 sampled echo and fallback rounds were produced by a language-model assistant, not by human raters, and
the manuscript says so. (iv) Exact acceptance bounds (5.8\% one-sided for 1 acceptance in 80 runs; the earlier 7\% was
the two-sided value), a paired comparison of the rubric-guided and rubric-free variants, a single-session rescoring of the
exploratory prompts, an intermediate open-weight refiner (Qwen3-8B, 1 of 9 runs changed the prompt), cost per
variant, and a list of all base prompts. (v) The title and claims now state the scope (Qwen3-1.7B, nine tasks, weak base
prompts), and ``pre-specified'' is replaced by ``protocol fixed in advance, with listed amendments''.

\medskip
What we could not do. We have no second target family, we could not rerun the strong closed and large refiners (no router
access at this time), and we collected no human ratings. The paper lists these limits.
